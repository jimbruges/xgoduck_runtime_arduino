Head and mouth

Head 1–4 are neck pitch, head pitch, head yaw and head roll (IDs 30–33), each ±1. In Default pose they move those joints directly, by up to ±30°, ±35°, ±60° and ±20°. While walking they are inputs to the walk policy, which decides how far the head follows. The mouth is motor 34, 0–30°, and is sent with the 50 ms command heartbeat. It is not part of the policy observation.

Walk tuning —

Settings for matching this robot to the walking policy. Each is saved on the robot (data/tuning.json) and applied immediately; the defaults reproduce the original runtime. Change one thing at a time and compare with Last walk. The battery figure above is the lowest servo supply voltage and the hottest servo: the XGO policies were trained at 7.4–8.0 V, so it turns amber below 7.4 V, where torque and walking degrade.

Walk policy
What
The ONNX walk model the robot runs. Any python/xgoduck_walk*.onnx on the robot can be chosen.
Why
The original xgoduck_walk.onnx was trained on a model without the battery and Uno Q at the back. xgoduck_walk_realcom.onnx was retrained (jimbruges/xgoduck_rl, branch real-mass-com) with the real 825 g mass, the centre of mass 8 mm back, the ±15 mm randomisation of current xgoduck_rl, and the 8–24 ms command delay of this runtime.
Tune
Compare both with the same walk and Last walk. Use Home pose: Model, so each policy uses the home it was trained with (22° for the retrained one). A retrained policy may need less or no Home lean. Switch only while not walking.
Home pose (observations and actions)

—

What
The joint pose the policy measures its observations from and adds its actions to.
Why
Each ONNX model records the home it was trained around (default_joint_pos): walk, get-up and pick 20°, roulade 24°. The original runtime used one hand-tuned 23° for all of them, so walk saw its hips and ankles 3° off. Model uses each policy's own trained home.
Tune
Prefer Model. Switch only while not walking.
Home lean · standing and forward
0°
Home lean · walking backward
0°
What
Stands the hip pitch and ankle home angles this many degrees more upright (feet stay flat), moving the body forward over the feet by about 1.3 mm per degree. The applied lean blends from the first value to the second as the walk command goes from 0 to −0.2 backward, at most 20°/s; Default pose uses the first.
Why
The XGO training model leaves out the Uno Q, shield and battery and puts their weight near the body's centre. On this robot they sit at the back, putting the real centre of mass an estimated 7–11 mm behind the trained one, beyond the ±3–8 mm the policy was trained to tolerate. It falls backward walking backward. Measured: backward walking needed 9–10° of lean, which made forward walking unstable, hence a separate backward value.
Tune
Forward: the most lean that keeps forward walking steady (start at 0). Backward: raise until backward walking holds (about 9–10° here). The real fix is moving the battery forward or retraining with the true centre of mass.
IMU level
—
What
The IMU's mounting roll and pitch, cancelled before the tilt estimate the policy uses for balance.
Why
Training randomised the IMU mount by up to 6° but expects a systematic board tilt to be corrected on the robot. Measured with a spirit level, this robot's IMU sits about 4° nose-up.
Tune
Torque off, hold the robot so a spirit level (or phone level app) on the body's flat top reads level both ways, keep still, press Level IMU. Not in Default pose: head weight and servo sag tilt the body there, and levelling that hid a real backward lean and made it fall backward.
Command smoothing
off
What
A low-pass on the walk and head commands (time constant), so stick and key changes reach the policy as ramps.
Why
Policies are trained on smoothly varying commands; a gamepad gives steps. Upstream Microduck smooths its commands the same way.
Tune
0.1–0.2 s. Longer feels sluggish to the sticks.
Servo stiffness · KP
6
Servo damping · KD
20
What
Run gains of every joint servo in Default pose and Walk (temporary KP/KD registers), applied live and confirmed by the MCU. Defaults KP 6, KD 20.
Why
The flight recorder shows the legs reach only about 40–50% of each commanded swing, about 85 ms late; the policy then over-corrects. Training assumed servo P = 5 and a 4 ms command delay. Upstream Microduck runs its servos with no damping, because damping makes them slow.
Tune
One step at a time (KP +2, or KD −6), Clear, walk, compare Last walk. Stop if a servo buzzes, the robot shakes standing, or a temperature climbs past about 50 °C.
Last walk (flight recorder)
—
What
For walking forward, backward, turning and standing: the follow ratio (achieved over commanded leg swing, 1.0 = all of it) and the delay, plus falls and resting pitch. Covers all walking in the last 20 s and updates every 2 s.
Why
This is how the problems above were found; it turns "it falls" into numbers you can compare between settings.
Tune
Clear before each change so the numbers only cover the new setting.

Action smoothing

What
Low-pass on the policy's output actions: each tick keeps α of the previous action. 0 = no filtering.
Why
Kept at the reference deployment's 0.45. Upstream Microduck disagrees with itself here (its training repo says policies are trained unfiltered; its runtime filters with 0.7 legs / 0.5 head), and the XGO training repo doesn't say. Filtering adds delay on top of the servos' ~85 ms.
Tune
If walking is sluggish or late to catch a stumble, try lowering Leg α (0.3, then 0.15); if it jitters, raise it.
